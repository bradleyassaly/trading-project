# Copy Trading Analysis — 2026-04-11

Follow-up to `whale_entry_experiments_2026-04-11.md`. The previous session sliced whale_entry by tier, slippage, entry-price zone, convergence, hold duration, conviction, and category, and found zero statistically significant positive-EV configurations across 122 grid combinations. This session asks the deeper question: **is the loss driven by copying the wrong individual wallets, or is copy trading fundamentally broken?**

The answer is the latter, more decisively than the previous session.

## Executive Summary

- **Is per-wallet copy trading viable? No.**
- Across 87 resolved whale_entry signals from **only 9 unique wallets** (with n ≥ 2), 4 wallets have positive EV and 5 are negative.
- The 4 "positive" wallets have **near-zero EV (+0.0004 to +0.012)**. They win frequently but only by tiny margins. None of them is generating real edge.
- The 5 "negative" wallets are catastrophic — average EV **−0.60** per signal.
- Cherry-picking the absolute best 3 (wallet, category) cells produces aggregate EV **+0.0009** at n=46. Statistically "significant" (p < 0.001) but **economically meaningless** — one-tenth of one percent per trade, well below fees + slippage + adverse selection.
- Whitelist file was generated by the script and then **deleted** because it would mislead someone into thinking copy trading works.

## The Distribution Is Not Bimodal

The original hypothesis was that aggregate −0.20 EV hides a mix of "copyable winners" and "toxic losers," and finding the winners would unlock the strategy. The data rejects this:

| group | wallets | total_ev | avg ev per signal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| positive-EV wallets | 4 | **+0.14** | **+0.004** |
| negative-EV wallets | 5 | **−16.95** | **−0.599** |

The "winners" cluster *just above zero*, not in a separate positive mode. Their wins are real (4 of 4 have WR ≥ 50%, two have 100% WR) but the dollar-amounts they win by are tiny — they're betting at extreme prices (>0.85) where the upside per dollar is single-digit cents. The losers are betting in the uncertain zone and getting destroyed.

This is the same pattern the previous experiment surfaced as "entry price > 0.85 wins 79% but +0.002 EV." It now resolves clearly: **copying whales who bet at 0.90 captures zero edge, copying whales who bet at 0.50 destroys capital.** There is no middle.

## The Whitelist (Generated, Then Deleted)

The script ran a sweep over `(min_n, min_wr, exclude_bots)` and found 6 statistically-significant positive-EV configurations. The "best" by EV:

| min_n | min_wr | excl_bots | cells | agg_n | agg_wr | agg_ev | p_value |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 60% | True | 3 | 46 | 80.4% | **+0.0009** | <0.001 |

The whitelist would have been:
1. `0x07e78f5f58... × other` (n=18, WR 100%, EV +0.0015)
2. `0xb6bed94e75... × geopolitics` (n=3, WR 100%, EV +0.0010)
3. `0x23222038e8... × crypto` (n=25, WR 64%, EV +0.0004)

Aggregate EV is **+0.0009 per signal entry-price unit**. On a $100 trade, that's **+$0.09** per trade. After Polymarket fees (typically 1-2% of position size), spread (1-3%), and slippage (1-2%), the realised EV is **deeply negative**. There is no scenario in which this whitelist makes money once trading frictions are applied.

The whitelist file was written to `data/polymarket/copy_trading_whitelist.json` by the script and **immediately deleted**. Shipping a +0.09% EV whitelist would mislead future operators into thinking we have an edge we don't.

## Why The Tier-A Wallets Lose Money on whale_entry

This is the most informative single finding. The two A-tier wallets in the whale_entry sample lose −$7.14 and −$2.98 respectively. Three theories rank-ordered by evidence:

1. **Selection bias on signal-firing.** whale_entry probably fires when an A-tier wallet does something *atypical* (a freshness/novelty heuristic). Their *typical* trades — the ones their A-tier letter is built on — never trigger this signal. We're systematically copying their least-representative moves, which are exactly the ones with no edge.

2. **Signal type mismatch with arb-bot wallets.** `0x6d3c5bd139` is A-tier in `wallet_category_profiles` because it has high WR + high resolved count + decent total PnL. But its actual trading mode is **arb-bot** (652 trades/week, behavioral classification confirms `arb_bot`). Arb bots win by closing tight spreads on resolved-soon markets, not by directional alpha. Copying their fills means buying when they close a 0.51 ↔ 0.52 spread, which captures zero of the spread for us and exposes us fully to the underlying outcome risk.

3. **The wallet selection list is too narrow.** Only 9 wallets generated all 87 whale_entry signals over the sample window. This means the signal engine is firing on a small fraction of tracked wallets, repeatedly. Whatever bias selected those 9 wallets is the bias dominating the EV. Fixing this would require either (a) widening the trigger criteria so more wallets fire, or (b) changing what "qualifies" as a copyable trade so the *typical* moves of high-tier wallets get caught instead of their outlier moves.

## Recommendations

1. **Disable `whale_entry` from the executor path.** Add it to `DISABLED_SIGNAL_TYPES` in `whale_signal_engine.py:40`. Continue recording into `signal_outcomes` for ongoing monitoring — if a future ingestion expansion reveals new wallet behavior, the data will be there to re-evaluate.
2. **Audit `oversized_bet`'s disabled status.** It's currently disabled with the comment "negative edge in data" but live-readiness data shows the opposite (n=47, WR 85%, EV +0.37). The comment is stale. Re-enable.
3. **Build `accumulation × geopolitics` as the priority lane.** That single cell — `accumulation` signal type, `geopolitics` category — is the strongest in the entire signal_outcomes table at n=22, WR 91%, EV +0.41. Weight Kelly sizing toward it; consider relaxing the confidence floor for this slice specifically.
4. **Widen the whale_entry firing criteria** as a separate experiment. The current 9-wallet repeat-fire pattern is the root structural bias. Instead of trying to filter from 87 noisy samples, add wallets to the trigger pool by lowering the freshness/novelty bar and re-running this analysis at n=200 in 2-3 weeks.
5. **Investigate why active political/geo whales are missing from whale_entry.** `0xfedc381bf3`, `0xbaa2bcb543`, `0x9c667a1d1c`, `0xfa21179f2c` are A-tier in politics/geopolitics with recent activity, but **none** of them appear in the 9-wallet whale_entry sample. Whatever code path qualifies wallets for whale_entry is filtering them out. That's a bug or a stale config — and fixing it might unlock the political alpha that's been promised but not delivered by this signal type.
6. **Re-run this analysis at n=200.** If the verdict is unchanged, **permanently retire whale_entry**. If new wallets enter the sample and produce different per-wallet results, the structural fix in #5 is doing real work.

## Final Verdict on Copy Trading Viability

Per-wallet copy trading on the existing whale_entry signal does not have a recoverable edge. The "winning" wallets win at near-zero EV; the losing wallets lose catastrophically; statistical significance is achievable only on cells whose economic significance is below break-even.

The structural signals — `accumulation`, `market_maker_flip`, and (re-evaluated) `oversized_bet` — are where the alpha lives. Continuing to operate `whale_entry` in any form pollutes the signal pipeline and consumes paper bankroll. **Disable it from execution; keep the recording for the audit trail; redirect engineering effort to widening the active signal types that actually work.**
